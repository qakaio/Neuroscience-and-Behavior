## **1\. Abstract**

This proposal begins with the hypothesis that human behavior may, under certain circumstances, be influenced by factors extending beyond the mechanisms traditionally considered part of autonomous brain function.

The hypothesis considers the possibility, not currently demonstrated, that technologies unknown or not publicly documented could interfere directly or indirectly with processes associated with human cognition, including attention, decision-making, perception, preferences, behavior, and thought formation.

One speculative possibility is the existence of technologies developed by military, governmental, private, or other organizations capable of producing some form of neurocognitive modulation remotely or through mechanisms that are not currently understood.

This proposal **does not claim that such technologies exist**. It establishes their possible existence as a speculative hypothesis that would require independent, reproducible, and verifiable empirical evidence before being considered scientifically plausible.

---

## **2\. Central Problem**

Contemporary neuroscience describes human behavior as the result of interactions among neural processes, physiological state, genetics, previous experiences, sensory stimuli, environmental conditions, social context, and numerous other factors.

This hypothesis questions whether these mechanisms necessarily represent the complete set of factors capable of influencing human behavior.

The central question is:

> **Is it possible that external mechanisms, currently unknown or insufficiently characterized, could influence human neurocognitive processes in a systematic manner?**

A secondary question is:

> **If such an external mechanism existed, would it be possible to experimentally distinguish its effects from known psychological, social, environmental, biological, and technological influences?**

---

## **3\. Primary Hypothesis**

**H1.** Human cognitive and behavioral processes may, in principle, be influenced by external factors that have not yet been identified by current science.

A stronger and more speculative version would be:

**H2.** A technology may exist that is capable of producing neurocognitive modulation in humans without the individual's conscious awareness of the intervention.

H2 is substantially more extraordinary than H1 and therefore requires proportionally stronger evidence.

---

## **4\. Possible Hypothetical Mechanisms**

Without assuming that any of these mechanisms actually exist, the hypothesis could theoretically involve:

* alteration of neural activity;  
* modulation of attention;  
* interference with perceptual processes;  
* alteration of emotional states;  
* modification of decision-making processes;  
* induction or suppression of specific behavioral patterns;  
* interference with electrophysiological or neurochemical processes;  
* an unknown interaction between technology and biological systems.

These mechanisms are **conceptual possibilities, not established mechanisms**.

Any specific mechanism would need to be operationally defined before it could be scientifically investigated.

---

## **5\. Hypothetical Origin of the Technology**

The hypothesis does not assume a specific origin.

Conceptual possibilities could include:

1. military development;  
2. governmental development;  
3. private-sector development;  
4. independent development by non-governmental groups;  
5. technology derived from scientific research that has not yet been publicly disclosed;  
6. a technology whose underlying nature is not currently understood.

Attributing a hypothetical technology to any of these organizations or categories should not itself be considered evidence of its existence.

---

## **6\. Relationship to Brain Autonomy**

The hypothesis does not necessarily require the brain to lose its autonomy.

A more moderate interpretation would consider the brain a highly influenceable system in which external stimuli can modify the probability of certain thoughts or behaviors without completely determining the resulting response.

Under this model:

**External influence → change in neural state → change in cognitive probabilities → behavior**

This differs conceptually from:

**External influence → absolute control → predetermined behavior**

The latter claim would require substantially stronger evidence.

---

## **7\. The Problem of Verifiability**

One of the greatest problems with the hypothesis is its current difficulty of falsification.

The absence of evidence does not demonstrate that the phenomenon exists. At the same time, the absence of evidence does not establish that it does not exist.

For this reason, the hypothesis should not use its own lack of evidence as evidence that the technology is secret.

For example:

> "We cannot find the technology because it is secret."

is not, by itself, a scientifically sufficient explanation.

A methodologically stronger formulation would be:

> "There is currently insufficient publicly available evidence to determine whether the hypothesized phenomenon exists."

---

## **8\. Necessary Predictions**

For the hypothesis to progress from speculation toward a scientific hypothesis, it would need to generate observable predictions.

For example:

* individuals exposed to the alleged mechanism should exhibit measurable changes;  
* such changes should occur at a frequency greater than chance;  
* the phenomenon should exhibit some temporal or dose-response relationship;  
* the phenomenon should demonstrate consistent patterns;  
* independent observers should be able to detect the effect;  
* controlled experiments should reproduce the findings;  
* established alternative explanations should be systematically evaluated and eliminated where appropriate.

Without predictions of this nature, the hypothesis remains primarily philosophical or speculative.

---

## **9\. Control of Alternative Explanations**

A responsible investigation would first consider established explanations.

These could include:

* cognitive biases;  
* expectation and suggestion;  
* placebo and nocebo effects;  
* stress;  
* sleep deprivation;  
* hormonal changes;  
* neurological or psychiatric conditions;  
* medications and psychoactive substances;  
* exposure to media and algorithmic systems;  
* social influence;  
* conditioning;  
* statistical coincidence;  
* retrospective memory distortion;  
* confirmation bias;  
* ordinary behavioral patterns.

A hypothesis involving an unknown technology should gain scientific credibility only if plausible conventional explanations are systematically investigated and found insufficient.

---

## **10\. Falsification Principle**

A scientifically useful version of the hypothesis must allow for the possibility that it is wrong.

Therefore:

> **If controlled experiments demonstrate that phenomena attributed to an alleged external influence can be sufficiently explained by known mechanisms, the hypothesis of a specific external technology should be rejected or substantially modified.**

This principle is essential to prevent the hypothesis from becoming impossible to challenge.

---

## **11\. Distinguishing Experience from Evidence**

A subjective experience may be real as an experience without necessarily identifying its correct cause.

For example, an individual may perceive an unusual change in their thoughts, behavior, or decision-making.

That experience constitutes a phenomenon that can potentially be studied.

However, the conclusion:

> "This change was caused by an external technology."

is an additional causal hypothesis requiring independent evidence.

This distinction should be maintained throughout any investigation.

---

## **12\. Current Status of the Hypothesis**

**Classification:** Speculative hypothesis.

**Direct evidence:** Not established.

**Mechanism:** Unknown.

**Technological origin:** Unknown.

**Experimental predictions:** Require further operationalization.

**Scientific status:** Undemonstrated.

The hypothesis should not currently be presented as a scientific discovery or established fact.

---

## **13\. Future Objective**

The objective of this proposal is not to establish in advance that a technology capable of modulating human cognition exists.

The objective is to establish a framework through which the following question can be investigated empirically:

> **Are there systematic cognitive or behavioral changes that cannot be adequately explained by known mechanisms and that provide independent evidence consistent with an unknown external factor?**

If the answer is negative, the hypothesis should be weakened or abandoned.

If the answer is positive, the phenomenon must first be identified, characterized, and independently reproduced before attributing it to a specific technology.

---

## **14\. Epistemological Principle**

The hypothesis must remain separate from the conclusion.

**Hypothesis:** An unknown external influence could modify human cognitive processes.

**Possible conclusion:** Only reproducible evidence could justify accepting the hypothesis.

**Not sufficient evidence:** lack of an explanation, an isolated subjective experience, coincidences, retrospectively perceived patterns, or the inability to access classified information.

The purpose of this document is therefore to transform a speculative suspicion into an investigable question while preserving both the possibility of confirmation and the possibility of falsification.

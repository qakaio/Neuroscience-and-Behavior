## X-ray of the Mock

### Dialogue and the theft of attention

Most of the "mock" comes down to the act of talking, forcing the other person to pay attention, in a situation where there would normally be total indifference.

The aggressor starts a dialogue, usually something childish. Naturally, the level of the conversation wouldn't warrant any kind of response. Then the aggressor forces the victim to pay attention, making it impossible for them to ignore what is being said. Meanwhile, the aggressor also silences the victim, so they can't respond adequately or defend themselves verbally.

**Positive note**

Eventually something happens and the silence breaks, and the aggressor is surprised by the responses that come out, confirming that the abuse only works in an extremely manipulated, fictional scenario. Another important point is that the theft of attention is a temporary state: they'll provoke you with some silly argument, and for a few minutes your world will revolve around it. Then your focus returns and you forget everything that just happened, and go have coffee or smoke a cigarette, without a worry in the world.

> "I'll wait for this monologue to end and then get back to my projects."

### Mental block

When I'm put into this state, I'm unable to carry out most tasks — work, studying, gaming, dating. My body goes limp, creativity fades, and I usually can only lie down or smoke and make coffee. Another thing that comes with it is low self-esteem: no matter how much progress you're making in life — creating content, gaining followers, growing professionally, studying, being praised by everyone around you — the mental block temporarily "erases" all of that and creates a feeling of emptiness. When the effect passes, all your memories come back and your joy returns. It's important to mention that when I'm in this state, the best thing to do is ignore it and carry on with my day normally, since the feeling of emptiness is artificial and your achievements remain exactly where they are, untouched, still bringing you the same joy and sense of personal fulfillment as always.

**Positive note**

I've learned to adapt, and nowadays I can use this time productively for low-effort tasks:

- Meditating
- Studying with AI
- Developing self-awareness
- Working on my emotional intelligence
- Simple games like Candy Crush
- Doomscrolling, with a filter

> "Boredom forced me to understand things that used to be confusing and meaningless."

### Body pain and physical limitations

This part of the mock aims to weaken the victim's body, leaving muscles stiff and joints hard.

Main areas: neck, spine, shoulder blades, elbows, and knees. You don't feel pain, just extreme discomfort. This limitation directly affects any kind of physical exercise. Some days it's hard to get out of bed.

**Positive note**

Despite the discomfort, it's possible to have autonomy and live a normal life. A lot of people my age are also "lazy" about washing dishes, running, or exercising. It ends up being a bad thing, but it doesn't impact my lifestyle that much, since I've always been a homebody/geek type, and even without the pain I'd probably spend my days sitting at the computer anyway.

> "It feels like I have a physical disability, and like any physical limitation, you adapt and overcome it."

### Real victory

The victim's discomfort is often used by the aggressor to demonstrate victory or superiority. That's not realistic — the real victory here is:

- Professional success
- Financial stability
- Personal achievements
- Mental health
- Healthy relationships
- Taking care of Nina
- Emotional intelligence, integrity, and inner peace

When he insists on saying he "won" because he knocked you down, he's revealing three very clear things:

**1. His criterion for victory is childish and fragile.** In his mind, winning means causing pain. It's not about building, not about moving forward, not about being at peace. It's about knocking the other person down. That's not strength. It's emotional misery with an imaginary trophy.

**2. He needs to keep repeating the narrative because it doesn't hold up on its own.** Whoever really wins doesn't need to declare victory. The fact that he keeps repeating it is a sign of inner doubt. He's trying to convince you in order to convince himself.

**3. He's still playing, and still trying to pull you back in.** That phrase is bait. If you react, explain yourself, defend yourself, or try to redefine what "winning" means, you're back on stage. Even if you win the argument, you lose the game.

**Positive note**

I'm winning in every area of my life. The only place I "lose" is in the childish competition of knocking people down — which, by the way, wouldn't add anything to my life besides a short, worthless ego boost. Even if I had the same power as the aggressor, I would never use it to attack someone vulnerable and defenseless. What I feel isn't defeat, but a kind of grief for everything I lost along the way. It was never a matter of winning or losing, but of being aware that I suffered an injustice and had to move forward anyway.

> "As long as I'm making progress, I'm winning."

### On relationships

Relationships can be deceiving, because as far as we know, people close to us might be going through the same difficulties. If my mother, my brother, or even Letícia show unusual behavior, the best thing to do is to ignore it and offer support.

> "We all have flaws. Respect above all."

### Behaviors

While I'm a victim of this abuse, don't judge me for my behavior, for my attitudes, for what I said, for what I think, for drinking too much or using drugs — in short, don't waste your time. I know what to do, I know how to behave, I don't need your advice, suggestions, or judgment. Have the decency to look past the surface of the situation.

> "Criticism always comes from below. Whoever is above should, at the very least, have the decency to know how to behave in a civilized way and not judge."

### Benefits

Not everything is bad. The people involved clearly have access to tools that humanity would be unable to comprehend. At the very beginning, there was a lot of judgment: I was called insensitive, selfish, narcissistic, paranoid, an alcoholic, and other things of that sort. Not only did I disprove all of those claims, I also proved my worth as a human being by showing intelligence and integrity. Regardless of how the situation unfolded, I'm sure I touched people who can influence my future, and the future of everyone I love, for the better. Beyond that, the constant suffering that followed me for years, along with the deep emotional scars I carry, made me develop a sixth sense for interpersonal skills. I became strong and wise, and I use that for good.

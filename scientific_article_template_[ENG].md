## 1. Introduction

The relationship between sleep and cognition is widely documented in the literature, but much of the research focuses on sleep *quantity* (hours slept), overlooking qualitative markers such as sleep efficiency, sleep-onset latency, and sleep fragmentation (Smith & Jones, 20XX). Understanding how these specific markers relate to concentration metrics is relevant both to cognitive neuroscience and to practical applications in educational and workplace settings.

### 1.1 Research Problem

[Describe the gap in the literature. Example:] Although there is consistent evidence that sleep deprivation impairs sustained attention, few studies separately examine the components of sleep *quality* (efficiency, latency, nighttime awakenings) as independent predictors of cognitive performance.

### 1.2 Objectives

- **General objective:** To investigate the correlation between sleep-quality markers and concentration metrics.
- **Specific objectives:**
  1. Measure objective sleep markers via actigraphy (efficiency, latency, fragmentation).
  2. Assess concentration performance using standardized cognitive tests.
  3. Analyze the strength and direction of correlations between these variables.

### 1.3 Hypotheses

- **H1:** Higher sleep efficiency is associated with better performance on sustained-attention tasks.
- **H2:** Greater sleep fragmentation is associated with a higher error rate on concentration tasks.
- **H0 (null):** There is no significant correlation between the sleep markers and concentration metrics assessed.
